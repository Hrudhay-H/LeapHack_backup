The method

The baseline compares a field with itself.

Compared with itself

Each pixel is scored against the median and spread of its own field. Sensors and lighting differ between farms, and a reference learned at one site did not carry over.

A floor, not a ranking

Below a set deviation nothing is flagged. A healthy field returns “no significant anomaly” instead of a forced top five percent.

A second look, held lightly

A pretrained model checks canopy texture. It was near chance on real maize and rice, so it carries 30% of the fused score.

05 / 14

Three design decisions, each forced by something we saw in real data. 1. Compared with itself. A robust median and MAD z-score within the field. We tried the alternative: a reference learned at the healthy rice site, applied to the blight site. It did not transfer. Exposure, ground cover and date differ between sites, so absolute thresholds break. 2. A floor, not a ranking. If we flagged the top few percent of pixels, every field would have hotspots. With an absolute floor, a healthy real rice block and a real maize field both come back as “no significant anomaly”. That is a valid result, not a failed scan. 3. A second look, held lightly. The visual texture model (PatchCore on a pretrained ResNet-18) was near chance on real maize (0.56 to 0.66 AUROC) and on rice blight severity (0.54). We kept it as an experimental second opinion. Its 30% weight was chosen after seeing results on our planted patches, so it is provisional, and the limits slide says so.
