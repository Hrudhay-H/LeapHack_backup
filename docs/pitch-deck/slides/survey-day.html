Live demo cue: open /admin, then Survey-day simulator, then Start survey day. The next survey arrives for three farms and runs through quality checks, analysis, hotspot extraction, a PDF report and an SMS to the farmer, in about ten seconds. Each farmer's dashboard then opens on the new survey, with the earlier one still selectable. The story. Kikkeri already had a survey on record three weeks ago with one low-priority hotspot; the problem has now grown, spread to small satellite spots and been joined by a new patch. Maddur was clean last time and now has an elongated strip and a patch. Pandavapura is a newly added farm with a clean first survey. What is real and what is simulated, say it plainly. The imagery is real: a rice research station from a public UAV dataset (CC BY 4.0), cut into blocks. The damage is simulated: we drew irregular, patchy damage with thinning into the real bands at known locations, so we can check what the pipeline finds and misses. It is not real pest damage. We did this because the real blighted plots in that dataset looked the same as the grass around them in our analysis, so there was no real anomaly this pipeline could find. The result, as it is. Kikkeri: 2 of 4 simulated patches had at least 30 percent of their area flagged, and 34 percent of all simulated damage was flagged; the two small satellite spots were missed. Maddur: 2 of 2, and 76 percent of the damage flagged. About 99 to 100 percent of the flagged area lies inside a simulated patch, so false alarms were rare. Hotspots show LOW confidence because the dataset does not state its radiometric calibration. Damage strength was chosen so it is clearly visible in RGB, not tuned to the detector.
