How it works

From drone survey to scouting list

1

Survey in

RGB, or RGB plus multispectral, aligned onto one georeferenced grid.

2

Check and mask

Quality checks, then a crop mask that drops paths, bunds and bare ground.

3

Two signals

A vegetation index against the field itself, and an experimental texture check.

Fuse and rank

Index-led fusion, hotspot polygons, area, priority, confidence and a pattern hint.

This is a real sequence, so the numbers mean something. Walk it left to right with the image: a survey goes in, a ranked set of numbered hotspots comes out. Step 2 matters more than it looks. The crop mask chooses its vegetation threshold from the survey itself because absolute NDVI differs between sensors (we saw 0.44 on a rice site and 0.3 to 0.7 on maize). It also closes narrow gaps and fills small enclosed ones, so a badly damaged patch inside a plot is not dropped as non-crop. Damage that opens wide onto a path still looks like bare ground and can be missed. Step 3: the vegetation index is the dependable signal. The texture check is a pretrained model used as a second opinion and is marked experimental everywhere in the product. Works with RGB-only drones too: without a multispectral file it uses a VARI colour index instead of NDVI and NDRE.
